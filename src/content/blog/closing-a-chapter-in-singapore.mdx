Singapore and I go back to 2012. I came for college, worked here after, then
spent three years in Vietnam before coming back in 2024 when I joined Meta. The
apartment was only twenty months old, but the country holds a lot more than
that. So when I flew back two weeks ago to empty the place and break the lease
early, the last piece of paperwork after
[a layoff](/blog/layoff-and-a-newborn/) and the visa that came with it, I
expected a bit of admin and some cleaning. It turned out to be a goodbye.

The last time I left, part of me assumed I'd be back. I wasn't married, nothing
was settled, and the door stayed open. This time is different. Walking through
those rooms, I knew I wasn't coming back, and that most of what I was doing over
those two weeks I was doing for the last time.

When Meta relocated us, they shipped everything we owned across from Vietnam,
so the apartment was full, and none of it was making the return trip. I spent
my days taking it apart, and I kept catching myself standing over some small
thing, trying to remember when we bought it, which shop, which afternoon.

The kitchen was the worst of it. Every bowl and plate and knife came with a
picture attached: my wife cooking dinner, baking the cake she knows I love, the
two of us at the sink doing dishes after a meal. The rice cooker, the little
jars of condiments, the pots and pans we must have cooked thousands of meals
in. I put all of it in the bin anyway. In the storage room I found the dried
food she'd carried over from home, most of it expired now, and I found myself
guessing which trip back to Vietnam each bag had come from. Even her plastic
bags got to me, the ones she folds into neat little squares after every grocery
run and saves for later. I used a good number of them hauling things out, so at
least those got used the way she meant them to.

The bigger things went too. I gave away the desk and chair I'd worked from for
four years, sold the bed and mattress we'd slept on for eighteen months. Even
the slippers we brought over from Vietnam, cheap as they are, brought a memory
back before I threw them out.

The fish tank was the hardest. That corner was where I sat for hours after
work with my hands in the water, and it was the one place a bad day would
quietly loosen its grip. Taking it apart was slow work: draining the bucket,
lifting out the corals, throwing away every bottle of water treatment. I
fed them one last time before that. They came up to the surface the way they
always do, probably for the food and not for me, but it still felt like they
were seeing me off.
Late that night, so nobody would ask, I scooped them out and carried them to an
outdoor tank a few doors down that belongs to someone else in the
neighborhood. I slipped them in and left a bottle of their food beside it. I
hope they settle in, and I hope whoever finds them is kind. I'll miss you guys.

On the last night I met the friends I've known for almost a decade. We had a
few beers and watched a World Cup match, the way we always used to, and for a
couple of hours it was easy to forget I was leaving. When it was time to say
goodbye it was pouring, and we ran through the rain to catch a cab. That's the
kind of thing that stays with you longer than it should.

And under all of it, I missed my son. [Voi](/blog/welcome-voi/) wasn't there
for any of this, and I felt his absence the whole time. In a way that made the
leaving easier: I was packing up one place so I could go be with him. I don't
know yet what the next chapter looks like, but I know how I want to spend the
months right in front of me, and that's giving him everything I've got.
Singapore was good to me for a long time, and I'm grateful for it. But it's
time to go home. Goodbye.
