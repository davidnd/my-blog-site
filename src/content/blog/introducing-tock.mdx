Every day there's a pile of small things I want to do later. Review John's PR in
thirty minutes. Reply to that message after lunch. Check the deploy before I log
off. None of them are hard. The problem is I'm in the middle of something else
when they show up, and setting a proper reminder means stopping what I'm doing.

On my Mac that's open Reminders, make a new one, type the title, find the date
field, pick a time, close it. On my phone it's about the same, plus unlocking it
and getting distracted by whatever else is on the screen. It's not a lot of steps,
but it's enough that I usually don't bother. I tell myself I'll remember. I don't.

So I made [Tock](/products/tock/). Press ⌃⌘R from any app, type "review John's PR
in 30m", hit Enter. That's it. Thirty minutes later it pops up, I do the thing,
I mark it done. The whole setup takes about five seconds and I'm back to what I was
doing before I've lost my train of thought.

You write the time however it comes out of your fingers. "In 30m", "in 30min" and
"in 30 minutes" all mean the same thing. "Take the tea bag out in 90s" works as a
quick timer. "Call mom tomorrow at 5", "team lunch this Friday at noon", "pay rent
on the 1st" all do what you'd expect. There's no syntax to learn, and it shows you
what it understood before you press Enter, so there's no guessing.
It lives in the menu bar, has no account, no sync, and doesn't touch the network.

It's most useful when you're juggling a few things at once and want to push one
of them off without losing it, or when a thought hits you mid-task and you just
need to get it out of your head. That's most of my day, so I use it constantly.

I've been using it for a while, actually. I wrote the first version in April
2023, by hand, back before anyone was vibe coding anything. It was called MiniMind
then. I wanted to learn how desktop apps work, and coming from JavaScript, Electron
was the obvious pick. Slack and a lot of other apps were built on it at the time.
My wife and I have used MiniMind ever since, but it stayed just ours because of
the UI. I'm not a frontend person, and no matter how much time I put into it, it
looked like it was made by a backend engineer, because it was.

This year I asked Claude to redesign it, and the result is far more modern and
polished than anything I would have made on my own. It's the first version I've felt good about
showing other people. It needed a new name too, since I recently found out MiniMind
is now an open-source project for training small LLMs. That project came after my
app, but it has a lot more users than my two. So I asked Claude for a name as well,
and it suggested Tock. There are a few other apps called Tock, but it fits, so I
kept it.

Then I asked Claude to rewrite it in native Swift. It did it in one shot, and
the only things I had to fix were a few minor UI bugs. The Electron version was a
90MB download and took up about 230MB once installed, for an app that sets
reminders. The Swift version is a 1.5MB download and 7MB installed. I still
find that a little hard to believe.

The funny part is that the same wave that made Tock good enough to share also made
it a little less necessary. In 2023 you couldn't ask Siri for any of this. With
macOS 27.2 and the new Siri and Apple Intelligence, you can. Tell it to remind you
to review John's PR in 30 minutes and it will.

I still reach for Tock, for two reasons. The first is speed. Siri has to run what
you said through a language model before anything happens, and in my tests that
took up to ten seconds. Tock doesn't call a model or touch the network, so the
reminder exists the moment I press Enter. The second is the preview. Tock shows
what it understood before anything is saved, so if it got something wrong I fix it
before I commit. Siri creates the reminder first and tells you after. A few times it
got it wrong, and I had to go into Reminders and delete the bad one myself, which is
exactly the kind of fiddling I made Tock to get away from.

Tock is free and runs on macOS 13 or later. You can grab it on
