---
title: "Are we cooked?"
description: "Almost everything I believed about AI and software engineering has been wrong, and the disproving keeps accelerating."
pubDate: "2026-07-28"
tags: ["ai", "career", "software-engineering"]
---

We used to laugh at it. Someone would post a screenshot of a model confidently
inventing a library that doesn't exist, and we'd all pile on. Dumb autocomplete.
Stochastic parrot. Fun toy, not a threat. When people said AI would replace
software engineers, we argued back, and we won those arguments easily, because
the evidence was on our side. Two years after ChatGPT shipped, most of us still
felt safe, and that feeling was reasonable.

Then the last eighteen months happened. The last eight or ten in particular.

I have not written a meaningful line of code by hand in months. Not because I'm
lazy, but because it stopped making sense to. The model gets it right, and it
usually gets it right the first time. When it doesn't, one more sentence of
context fixes it. This is not a demo talking; this is my actual work, on real
systems, with real consequences when it breaks. The thing we called dumb is
now doing the job, and I'm one of the people who used to do that job.

The consolation we tell ourselves is that someone still has to steer. That's
true. Models need direction, taste, review, and someone who knows what a good
system looks like when the output compiles but the design is wrong. I believe
that. But notice how small that job is. Where a problem used to take ten
engineers, it now takes something closer to one. We keep repeating "humans in
the loop" as if it's a floor, when it might just be a smaller room.

Here's what I keep coming back to. It has been a year and a half. Not a decade,
not a generation. A year and a half, and nearly everything I confidently believed
about this has been disproved, one belief at a time, faster each time. My track
record on predicting the ceiling is perfect in the wrong direction. So when
someone now says software engineers will be replaced completely, I no longer have
a good reason to dismiss it. I used to think that argument was silly. Now I
just think it's early.

Three years is a long time at this rate. I don't know what's on the other side of
it. I don't think anyone does, including the people selling the models.

So: are we cooked? Maybe. Probably some of us. What I'm sure of is that the
version of this job I trained for is already gone, and the honest move is to stop
defending it and start paying attention to what's replacing it. I'd rather be
early to being wrong than late to being right.
